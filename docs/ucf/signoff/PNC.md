# Privacy: notice and consent

| UC-PNC-01 | **Notice with each consent request**<br>A stand-alone notice is shown before or together with every consent request. The notice lists the personal data collected, the purposes and what each purpose enables, how to withdraw consent and use rights, and how to complain to the Board. Each collection point is reviewed whenever it is added or changed, so that people can give informed consent. The approved notice and the consent screens are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>critical | List all consent collection points and sample a mix of channels, at least one web and one app or paper flow where they exist. For each, check the notice appears before or with the request and covers the data, purposes, withdrawal and rights route, and the Board complaint route. Pass if every sampled flow meets all points and the notice is readable without other documents. | Privacy notice; screenshots or recordings of every consent collection flow; list of consent collection points; notice approval record; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.5(1); DPDP Rules 2025 r.3-a,b,c,d,e,f (full) | DPDPA-only. Privacy-only. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-02 | **Notice and consent in the person's language**<br>Every notice and consent request can be read in English or in the person's choice of another officially listed language. The language options and their translations are kept current whenever a notice or consent screen changes. This lets people understand what they agree to in a language they use. The language selector and the translated texts are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>medium | Select a sample of consent flows and open each in English and in at least one other listed language. Check that both the notice and the consent text appear in the chosen language and match the current English wording. Pass if every sampled flow offers the choice and the translations are complete and current. | Language selector screenshots for each consent flow; translated notice and consent texts; translation review or approval record; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(1), 6(3)-e (full); s.5(1); DPDP Rules 2025 r.3-g (full) | Privacy-only. Number of languages is not settled by the text. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-03 | **Notice for previously collected data**<br>The people whose personal data was collected before the organization's current privacy duties began are identified. A notice is then sent to them as soon as reasonably practicable, describing the personal data held and the purposes it is used for. This brings older data under the same transparency as new data. The list of affected people, the notice and the dispatch log are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>medium | Obtain the list of people with older data and check how it was built. Sample recipients and trace each to a dispatch record showing date and channel. Check the notice text describes the data held and the purposes. Pass if the population is identified, the sampled recipients were notified, and the notice content is complete. | Register or extract of people with older consented data; retrospective notice text; dispatch log with dates and channels; plan or schedule for remaining recipients; covering the period since the duties began. | DPDPA: s.5(2) (full) | Privacy-only. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-04 | **Published privacy contact**<br>The contact details of the person who answers privacy questions appear in every notice and are shown prominently on the website and app. The details are checked whenever the contact person changes. This gives people a working route for questions about their data. The published pages and the notices are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>medium | Check the website, the app and the notice at each consent flow for the contact details. Send a test message to the published channel and confirm it reaches the named person. Sample responses to people's requests from the review period and check they include the contact. Pass if the contact is present, prominent, current and reachable. | Website and app screenshots showing the contact; current notice text; role or appointment record naming the contact person; sample responses to people's requests showing the contact; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(3), 8(9); DPDP Rules 2025 r.9 (full) | Privacy-only. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-05 | **Consent by clear affirmative action**<br>Consent is collected only through a clear affirmative action by the person. Screens carry no pre-selected options, never treat silence or continued use as consent, and show accept and decline with equal prominence. Each consent flow is reviewed when it is built or changed. This keeps consent real and not manufactured by design. The approved consent designs and live screen captures are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>critical | Capture or obtain dated screens for a sample of consent flows from the review period, covering each channel. Check there is an affirmative action, no pre-selected choice, no consent inferred from inactivity or continued use, and equal prominence for accept and decline. Pass if every sampled flow meets all points and matches the approved design. | Consent screen design standard; dated screenshots or recordings of every live consent flow; design approval records; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(1), 6(3)-a (full); s.6(1)-b (full) | Privacy-only. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-06 | **Consent given freely**<br>Consent is given freely. The service does not make access depend on consent to processing it does not need, and no consent request contains a term by which people waive their rights. Sign-up flows and linked terms are checked whenever they change. This stops people from being forced to trade away their data or rights. The consent screens, the terms and a decline walkthrough are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>high | Select a sample of services and run a sign-up that declines every optional purpose. Check the sign-up completes and the core service works. Read the terms linked from the consent flows for any waiver of rights. Pass if every sampled sign-up completes without optional consent and no waiver term is found. | List of processing needed for each service and optional processing; consent screens; terms of service and terms linked from consent flows; recorded sign-up walkthrough declining optional items; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(1), 6(3)-b (full); s.6(1)-a (full) | Privacy-only. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-07 | **Consent request states the purpose**<br>Every consent request states the specific purpose in clear and plain language, so that the person knows what they agree to. The wording of each request is reviewed when it is written or changed. This makes consent informed and not generic. The approved consent texts and live screens are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>high | Sample consent requests across channels from the review period. For each, check a specific purpose is named and the wording is plain and understandable without other documents. Pass if every sampled request states a specific purpose in plain language. | Consent request texts for every flow; wording review or approval records; dated screenshots of live consent screens; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(1), 6(3)-c,d (full) | Privacy-only. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-08 | **Purpose-level consent choices**<br>Each purpose appears as a separate item in the consent request, and the person can give or withhold consent for each one. A separate unticked control is built for every optional purpose. Refusing or withdrawing one purpose leaves the others unchanged. This allows real partial consent. The consent screens and the stored consent states are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>high | Select consent flows that serve more than one purpose. For each, check purposes are listed separately with separate unticked controls. Test one partial choice and confirm the other purposes are unaffected. Check the consent log holds people with mixed per-purpose states. Pass if every sampled flow offers separate choices and partial consent works. | Consent screens for flows with more than one purpose; design of per-purpose controls; consent log extract showing per-purpose states; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(1)-a (full); s.6(1) (full) | Privacy-only. Split from consent records (PNC-09): the test differs (consent screens versus stored records). | [x] |
| UC-PNC-09 | **Consent records**<br>Auditable consent records are kept. Each record shows the purpose, the date and time, the capture channel, the notice version shown, and any later withdrawal, stored separately for each purpose. The records are protected from alteration. This lets the organization prove consent when asked. The consent log and the notice archive are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>high | Select a sample of people across channels and ask for their consent records. Check each shows the purpose, date and time, channel, notice version and any withdrawal, with a separate status per purpose. Ask for the matching notice version for each. Pass if all sampled records are complete and every notice version is produced. | Consent log schema; consent log extract for a sample of people; notice version archive; withdrawal records; access control or change history for the log; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(1)-b (full); s.6 (full) | Privacy-only. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-10 | **Fresh consent for new purposes**<br>A trigger ensures that any new or changed purpose is reviewed before processing starts. Fresh notice and consent for that purpose are collected before the new processing begins. Existing consent is never reused for a purpose it did not cover. This keeps processing within what people agreed to. The change procedure and the new consent records are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>high | List new or changed purposes introduced in the review period and sample them. For each, compare the consent record date with the launch date of the new processing. Pass if every sampled purpose has fresh consent dated before the processing began, and the change procedure contains the trigger. | Change procedure with the privacy trigger; product or purpose change records; consent log extract showing new consent dates; launch dates for the new processing; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(1) (full) | Privacy-only. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-11 | **Consent withdrawal**<br>People can withdraw consent as easily as they gave it. Withdrawal takes no more steps and no less accessible a channel than giving consent, and the notice says how to do it. Give and withdraw flows are checked side by side whenever either changes. This keeps withdrawal from becoming a hurdle. The side-by-side flows are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>critical | Select a sample of consent flows across channels and run the give and the withdraw path for each. Count steps and compare the channels. Check the notice explains how to withdraw. Pass if withdrawal takes no more steps and no less accessible a channel than giving consent in every sampled flow. | Side-by-side recordings or screenshots of the give and withdraw flows in each channel; notice text on how to withdraw; counts of steps for each flow; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(4), 6(6)-a (full) | Privacy-only. Split from PNC-12: ease of the channel versus effect on processing. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-12 | **Processing stops after withdrawal**<br>Processing stops, and processors are made to stop, within a defined period after consent is withdrawn. The exception is processing the law requires or allows without consent. Stop instructions to processors are issued and recorded. This makes withdrawal have a real effect. The withdrawal log and the processor instruction records are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>critical | Sample withdrawals from the review period. For each, check the date of withdrawal, the date processing stopped in internal systems and the processor stop instruction and confirmation. Pass if every sampled withdrawal was applied within the defined period, including at processors, or has a recorded legal ground for keeping the data. | Withdrawal procedure stating the period; withdrawal log; processor instruction records and confirmations; records of retention grounds where the law requires keeping data; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(4), 6(6)-b (full) | Privacy-only. | [x] |
| UC-PNC-13 | **Consent manager use**<br>Consent actions are accepted through an intermediary only where the intermediary is a consent manager on the Board's published list. The organization's systems treat consent given, reviewed or withdrawn through the intermediary the same as direct actions. The intermediary's records are reconciled with internal consent status on a regular cycle. The agreement, the register extract and the reconciliation are the proof. | Data Protection Officer<br>medium | List every consent manager in use and check each against the Board's published list. Sample people from the review period and compare the manager's consent and withdrawal records with the internal consent log. Pass if every manager is listed and every sampled action, especially withdrawals, was applied internally. | Agreements with each consent manager; extract of the Board's published list showing each one; integration documentation; reconciliation of consent manager records against the internal consent log; covering the review period. | DPDPA: s.6(8)-(9) (full) | Privacy-only. Applies only where such intermediaries are used. | [x] |
