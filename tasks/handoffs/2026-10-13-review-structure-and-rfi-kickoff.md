## Part 1: the Review stage

### His model

- Review opens on a queue of what the assessment found.
- Approving a queue item turns it into a gap. That is the whole lifecycle.
- A gap can have a workpaper: how the conclusion was reached, what was seen in the policy and in the assessment and how each changed the conclusion, with links to the evidence.
- The Conclusions screen repeats what he already read and approved in the queue. So why four screens and not two?

### What the app does today (from template headings only; confirm before answering him)

Read `app/templates/pages/review_queue.html`, `conclusions.html`, `findings.html`, `workpaper.html` and their routes in `app/routers/web.py`, and open each on the demo app, before telling him anything. The previous session only read the headings:

| Screen | What it appears to be |
| --- | --- |
| Queue | Proposed conclusions awaiting approve, edit or reject. |
| Conclusions | Every control's approved conclusion, compliant ones included (93 for ISO in the demo). |
| Findings | A separate record made from an approved conclusion ("Approved conclusions without a finding", "New finding from ...", "First action"). It carries severity, status, owner and actions: remediation tracking. |
| Workpaper | A read-only record: analysis runs, a table of requirement, outcome and decision per framework, items excluded by scope, applicable requirements with no conclusion. |

If that reading holds, two things follow, and both should be put to him plainly:

- The workpaper is not what he described. It is an audit trail table, not an account of how each conclusion was reached. The narrative he wants (what the policy said, what the assessment found, how each moved the conclusion) is closest to the "reasoning on record" and "evidence cited" blocks in the finding detail, and no record of interviews exists.
- "Approved conclusion" and "finding" are two records today. In his model they are one thing: a gap. A compliant control produces an approved conclusion but no gap.

### The audit's own miss

The second-pass spec kept all four screens and gave them one shared detail layout (requirement R11). That made the repetition he noticed worse, not better. The audit classified his requirements but never asked whether the four screens should exist.

### A proposal to test with him (not decided)

Two screens in the Review step:

1. **Queue.** As approved. Items leave it when decided.
2. **Gaps.** The register of approved items that have a gap: the assessment sheet (domain, control, gap, risk, status), filterable to include compliant controls. Opening a gap shows its workpaper in his sense: the reasoning, the evidence with links, the history of decisions, and the remediation owner and actions if those stay.

Questions he must answer before anything is redrawn:

- Do compliant controls need a home in Review, or only in the report's auditor detail (which already lists all 93)?
- Do remediation owner, actions and status (today's Findings) stay in this product, and if so do they live on the gap or in a separate step after Report?
- Is the workpaper a per-gap section, or does he also need one whole-assessment workpaper document to export for an audit file?
- Is the trail that today's Workpaper holds (analysis runs, exclusions, unconcluded requirements) still needed somewhere? It may belong in Overview or the report's auditor detail.

Check the backend before promising anything: whether a finding is a separate table with its own lifecycle, and what the report, exports and remediation features read from it. Merging two records is a Track B (backend) change, not presentation. Section 6 of the plan must gain or lose rows accordingly.

Fixed decisions still apply: never two stacked navigation bars, stepper navigation, queue ordered pending first, FastAPI stays.
