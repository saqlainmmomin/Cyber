1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This policy sets out how Kestrel Ledger Pvt Ltd protects the confidentiality, integrity and availability of the information it holds on behalf of merchants, cardholders and its own staff. It applies to every employee, contractor and third party who uses company systems, whether they work from the Pune office, the Bengaluru operations centre or remotely. The policy covers the payments platform, the merchant dashboard, the internal finance systems and every cloud account the company operates. Where a local procedure conflicts with this policy, this policy prevails until the conflict is resolved by the information security team.
The company maintains an inventory of information assets that records each system, its business owner, the data it processes and its criticality rating. The inventory is held in the configuration management database and is reconciled against cloud billing records at the end of each month. Assets that cannot be matched to an owner are escalated to the head of engineering within ten working days.
2. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
The board of directors sets the risk appetite for information security and receives a written report on the security programme twice a year. The Chief Risk Officer approves this policy and any exception that exceeds the risk appetite. System owners are accountable for the security of the systems they own, including access decisions, patch status and the handling of audit findings. All production access is reviewed by the system owner every quarter and exceptions are recorded in the access register. Line managers make sure that their teams complete the mandatory security awareness module before they receive access to production data.
Staff must treat all data marked “Confidential” as restricted and must not share it outside the company’s approved systems. Personal devices may be used for email only when they are enrolled in the mobile device management service.
3. ACCESS CONTROL
3.1 Account Lifecycle
Every user account is linked to a named individual, and shared accounts are prohibited except for documented break-glass accounts held in the password vault. Joiners receive access based on the role profile approved by their line manager, and movers have access from their previous role removed within five working days. Leavers have all access revoked on their last working day, and the human resources system sends an automatic notification to the identity team.
Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for all remote access,	including vendor support sessions,
and is enforced by the identity provider.
Privileged access requires written authoriza-
tion from the information security team before it is granted. Privileged sessions on production databases are recorded, and the recordings are kept for one year. Administrators use separate privileged accounts that are not used for email or web browsing.
3.2 Passwords and Secrets
Passwords must be at least fourteen characters long and are checked against a list of known breached passwords when they are set. Application secrets are stored in the secrets manager, rotated at least every ninety days, and never committed to source code repositories. Automated scanning of every pull request blocks merges that contain credentials.
4. OPERATIONS SECURITY
Production changes follow the change management procedure, which requires a peer review, a successful automated test run and approval by the service owner. Emergency changes may be deployed first and reviewed within two working days, and each emergency change is reported to the change advisory board. Every ﬁrewall rule change is logged and reviewed by the network team within five working days.
Operating system and application patches rated critical are applied within fourteen days of release, and high-rated patches within thirty days. Vulnerability scans run weekly against all internet-facing systems and monthly against internal systems. Findings that cannot be fixed within the target time are recorded as risk exceptions with a named owner and an expiry date.
Anti-malware protection is installed on every laptop and server, and alerts are forwarded to the security operations mailbox. Backups of production databases are taken every six hours, encrypted, and copied to a second cloud region. A restore test is performed each quarter and the result is recorded in the operations log.
5. LOGGING AND MONITORING
Security-relevant events from the identity provider, the cloud control plane, the payment application and the databases are sent to the central log platform. Logs are protected against modification and retained for at least one year, with the most recent ninety days available for immediate search. Clocks on all systems are synchronised to the cloud provider's time service.
The security operations team reviews high-severity alerts within one hour during business hours and within four hours outside business hours. Alert rules are tuned every month to reduce false positives, and every change to an alert rule is peer reviewed. The annual security résumé report is presented to the board of directors each April.
6. INCIDENT MANAGEMENT
Any employee who suspects a security incident must report it to the security operations mailbox or the incident hotline without delay. The incident manager classifies each incident by severity and opens a record in the incident register. Incidents involving personal data are escalated to the Data Protection Officer immediately so that notification duties can be assessed.
For high-severity incidents the incident manager convenes a response team within two hours and provides status updates to the Chief Risk Officer every four hours. After each high-severity incident a post-incident review is held within ten working days and actions are tracked to closure in the risk register. The incident response plan is tested through a tabletop exercise at least once a year.
7. SUPPLIER SECURITY
Suppliers that process company or customer data are assessed before contract signature using the supplier security questionnaire. Contracts with such suppliers include confidentiality obligations, breach notification within twenty-four hours, the right to audit and return or deletion of data at the end of the contract. Critical suppliers are reassessed every year, and the results are reported to the risk committee.
Cloud service providers are selected from the approved list maintained by the procurement team. The company reviews the independent assurance reports of each critical cloud provider when they are published and records any exceptions that affect its own controls.
8. BUSINESS CONTINUITY
The business continuity plan identifies the payment authorisation service and the merchant settlement service as critical, with recovery time objectives of four hours and twenty-four hours respectively. Recovery procedures are documented for each critical service and are tested at least once a year. Test results and lessons learned are reported to the Chief Risk Officer.
The payment authorisation service is owned by the head of payments engineering and holds card transaction records. Backups of the payment authorisation service are taken every six hours and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the payment authorisation service is checked by the payments engineering lead, who signs the review record in the access management tool. Remote workers connect through the company virtual private network, which checks that the device is managed and that its disk is encrypted before a connection is allowed.
The merchant settlement service is owned by the head of settlement operations and holds merchant bank account details. Backups of the merchant settlement service are taken every twelve hours and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the merchant settlement service is checked by the settlement operations manager, who signs the review record in the access management tool. Guest wireless networks in company offices are separated from the corporate network and provide internet access only.
The merchant dashboard is owned by the head of product and holds merchant user profiles. Backups of the merchant dashboard are taken daily and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the merchant dashboard is checked by the product security champion, who signs the review record in the access management tool. Source code is held in the company code hosting service, where branch protection prevents direct changes to the main branch of any production repository.
The fraud scoring engine is owned by the head of risk analytics and holds device fingerprints and risk scores. Backups of the fraud scoring engine are taken daily and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the fraud scoring engine is checked by the risk analytics lead, who signs the review record in the access management tool. Test environments do not contain live cardholder data, and any data copied from production for testing is masked before use.
The customer support platform is owned by the head of customer operations and holds support tickets and call notes. Backups of the customer support platform are taken daily and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the customer support platform is checked by the support operations manager, who signs the review record in the access management tool. Encryption keys for the payment platform are managed in the cloud key management service, and access to key administration is limited to two named engineers.
The finance ledger is owned by the financial controller and holds general ledger entries. Backups of the finance ledger are taken every night and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the finance ledger is checked by the finance systems administrator, who signs the review record in the access management tool. Data in transit between services is protected with transport layer security, and connections that do not meet the minimum version are refused.
The human resources system is owned by the head of people and holds employee records. Backups of the human resources system are taken every night and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the human resources system is checked by the people operations lead, who signs the review record in the access management tool. Removable media is blocked on company laptops unless an exception is approved for a specific business need.
The data warehouse is owned by the head of data and holds aggregated transaction statistics. Backups of the data warehouse are taken weekly and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the data warehouse is checked by the data platform lead, who signs the review record in the access management tool. Printed documents containing confidential information are collected from printers immediately and disposed of in locked shredding bins.
The identity provider is owned by the identity team lead and holds user accounts and group memberships. Backups of the identity provider are taken daily and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the identity provider is checked by the identity engineering manager, who signs the review record in the access management tool.
The central log platform is owned by the security operations lead and holds security event logs. Backups of the central log platform are taken daily and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the central log platform is checked by the detection engineering lead, who signs the review record in the access management tool.
The code hosting service is owned by the head of engineering and holds source code and build pipelines. Backups of the code hosting service are taken daily and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the code hosting service is checked by the developer platform lead, who signs the review record in the access management tool.
The secrets manager is owned by the platform security lead and holds application credentials and keys. Backups of the secrets manager are taken every hour and are encrypted with keys that its owner cannot export. The access list for the secrets manager is checked by the platform security engineer, who signs the review record in the access management tool.
9. COMPLIANCE AND REVIEW
Compliance with this policy is checked through internal audits carried out by the internal audit function according to the annual audit plan. Breaches of this policy may lead to disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract. Exceptions to this policy must be requested through the exception form and are valid for a maximum of twelve months.
This policy is maintained by the information security team and approved by the Chief Risk Officer. The team checks the policy against changes in law, technology and the threat environment at least once a year. All production access is reviewed by the system owner every quarter and exceptions are recorded in the access register.
